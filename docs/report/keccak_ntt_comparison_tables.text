Keccak and NTT Comparison Tables

These tables are prepared for thesis/report slides. They intentionally separate
module-only results from full-accelerator/system results. This is important
because many Dilithium/ML-DSA papers report total accelerator Fmax and area, but
do not always isolate the Keccak or NTT block.


Table 1: Keccak / SHAKE Hardware Comparison

| Ref | Work / Year | Scope | Device | Keccak/SHAKE architecture | Fmax / Frequency | Area reported | Throughput / result | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | This work, 2026 | Module-only SHAKE128/256 | Cyclone V 5CGXFC7C7F23C8 | SHAKE-only Keccak-f[1600], 64-bit AXI, 2-stage round pipeline after optimization | 84.31 MHz | ~3,747 ALM, ~3,293 regs, 0 DSP, 0 BRAM | ~0.30 GB/s per lane for SHAKE128 after 2-cycle/round split; 208/208 UVM tests, 100% functional coverage | Direct thesis result; cleanly verified and synthesized on Cyclone V |
| K2 | Beckwith et al., 2021 | Full Dilithium accelerator | Artix-7 / Xilinx FPGA family | Multiple Keccak/SHAKE cores used for Dilithium hashing and sampling; 1 round/cycle style in the Keccak path | ~250 MHz class reported in Keccak branch notes / full-design literature | Keccak-only area not separated in our extracted notes | High-performance full accelerator baseline | Shows why multiple Keccak lanes are useful for ExpandA and signing |
| K3 | Zhao et al., 2022 | Full Dilithium accelerator | Artix-7 | KECCAK module supports SHAKE128 and SHAKE256 inside compact segmented-pipeline Dilithium architecture | 96.9 MHz full accelerator | 29,998 LUT, 10,366 FF, 11 BRAM, 10 DSP total design | Full design computes KeyGen/Sign/Verify for all security levels | Good compact full-accelerator reference, but not a clean Keccak-only comparator |
| K4 | ML-DSA-OSH, 2025 | Full ML-DSA open-source accelerator | UltraScale+ | FIPS-204 ML-DSA design based on prior Dilithium hardware; SHAKE integrated with NTT and sampler flow | 230 MHz clock setting for reported implementation | 54,942 LUT, 29,309 FF, 29 BRAM, 16 DSP total design | Open-source complete ML-DSA RTL; signing latency improved by scheduling | Best open-source full-design reference for future integration |
| K5 | LightHD, 2026 | Full Dilithium accelerator with Keccak submodule data | Artix-7 | Iterative dual-Keccak SHA-3/SHAKE module; two cores operate with 26-cycle offset; sampler consumes interleaved outputs | 212.8 MHz system-level reported result | Dual-Keccak block: 6,019 LUT, 3,220 FF total; individual cores 3,150 and 2,869 LUT, 1,610 FF each | Improves Keccak/sampler scheduling and reduces area vs prior dual-Keccak style | Most relevant current idea for future Keccak-sampler integration |
| K6 | Abdulsamad and Repas, 2026 | Module-only SHA-3 accelerator, not Dilithium-specific | Artix-7 | Fully pipelined Keccak-f[1600] core with loop unrolling, clock gating, and pipeline balancing | 210 MHz | Paper reports post-synthesis FPGA implementation; area/power optimized | 1.35 Gbps, 0.94 W, 1.44 Gbps/W | Useful module-level SHA-3 energy-efficiency comparator, but not SHAKE/Dilithium-specific |

Keccak table notes:

- K1 is the cleanest direct comparison for this thesis because it is our actual
  module-only synthesis result.
- K2 to K5 are Dilithium/ML-DSA accelerator papers. They are very relevant to
  architecture, but most do not report a fully isolated Keccak-only Fmax/area
  table.
- K6 is included because it is a Keccak/SHA-3 module-level paper, but it targets
  SHA-3 secure-network applications rather than Dilithium SHAKE/XOF workloads.
- The strongest thesis comparison sentence is:
  "Our Keccak engine is not yet the fastest per-core design, but it is
  SHAKE-specific, verified with UVM, synthesizes without DSP/BRAM, and its
  multi-lane structure directly matches Dilithium ExpandA parallelism."

NTT table notes:

- N1 is the thesis result and should be compared most directly against
  N5/N6/N7/N8/N9 because those are module-level NTT papers.
- Raw MHz comparison is not fair across FPGA families. Cyclone V is slower
  than Artix-7, UltraScale+, and Versal. The fair strengths of our NTT are
  cycle count, DSP count, deterministic memory-based scheduling, and UVM
  verification.
- Our main NTT slide claim should be:
  "The proposed NTT reaches about 299 cycles with only 6 DSPs on Cyclone V,
  placing its cycle count near modern fast NTT engines while maintaining very
  low DSP usage and complete verification."
- ParaPM is intentionally not included here because it is not a clean
  Dilithium NTT/full-accelerator comparator for this thesis table. It can be
  discussed separately as a polynomial-multiplier-related paper if needed.
